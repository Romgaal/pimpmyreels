# Hooks — advisory only

Say this **after** delivering the reel. Never block on it.

## The honest framing

Editing is not what drives views. In order of impact:

1. **The hook (0–3s)** — roughly 80% of the outcome
2. **Retention** — do they watch to the end
3. **Topic and niche** — saturation matters
4. **Account, algorithm, timing, sound**

Cutaway images help retention at the margin. They do not turn 1k into 10k. Tell the
user this plainly if they attribute view counts to the editing — it saves them from
optimizing the wrong thing.

## What to do

Read the first 3 seconds of the transcript. Then give:

- **A verdict** on the current opening: does it create tension, or does it merely
  describe the content? "3 tips for X" describes. It does not hook.
- **Two alternative hooks** to test:
  - **Counter-intuitive with a number** — "95% of the people you're afraid to
    approach are kind. Your brain is lying to you."
  - **Curiosity gap** — "3 things to remember to approach anyone — nobody tells you
    the third."

## Rules worth passing on

- The hook must be in the **first second**, spoken *and* on screen.
- **Never open with "3/3" or "part 2"** for a cold audience — they conclude they
  missed the beginning and scroll.
- **Trial reels reach a cold audience only.** They structurally get fewer views than
  normal reels. Compare trial to trial, never trial to normal, or the conclusion is
  garbage.
- One reel is one data point. Test 2–3 hooks before concluding anything.
